A home gym has a cord problem most rooms don't: the equipment moves, sweats, and sometimes gets stepped near at speed. Cord management here is less about aesthetics and more about not tripping over a treadmill's power cord mid-run.

## Keep Power Cords Off the Floor Path

Route the cord for a treadmill or bike along the wall behind the machine rather than letting it cross the floor space in front of or beside it — that's the area you're actually moving through, and a cord underfoot at speed is a real fall risk, not just clutter.

## Give Equipment Its Own Outlet Run

If more than one piece of equipment shares an outlet, a short surge-protected power strip mounted to the wall (not sitting on the floor) keeps the multi-plug cluster out of the walking area entirely. Floor-level power strips are the most common tripping hazard in home gyms.

## Plan for Sweat and Moisture

Cord covers and cable clips in a home gym should be a wipeable material — fabric sleeves absorb sweat and humidity over time. A plastic or silicone cord channel near equipment holds up better and is easier to clean.

## Phone and Charging Cords

If you charge a phone or tablet mid-workout, mount a short charging cord to the equipment frame or a nearby wall stand rather than leaving it loose on the floor — a charging cord underfoot is a smaller trip hazard than a power cord, but it's still one more thing in the way.

## The Takeaway

In a home gym, cord management is a safety task first and a tidiness task second. Get every cord off the floor space you actually move through, and the rest of the room's cable management can be as simple as anywhere else in the house.
