Bathroom outlets get treated differently from the rest of the house for a good reason — water and electricity in the same room raises the stakes on anything left plugged in or exposed. A few basics cover most of what actually matters.

## Confirm You Have GFCI Protection

A ground-fault circuit interrupter (GFCI) outlet — the kind with the small "test" and "reset" buttons — is required by code in most bathrooms built or renovated in the last few decades, and it cuts power almost instantly if it detects a ground fault. If your bathroom outlet doesn't have one, that's worth fixing before anything else on this list.

## Keep the Hair Dryer and Styling Tool Cords Managed

These are the highest-use, highest-wattage cords in the room, and they tend to end up looped over a towel bar or draped across the counter near the sink. A wall-mounted cord wrap or clip near the outlet keeps the cord off wet surfaces between uses.

## Cover Idle Outlets Around Kids

For a household with young kids, a spring-loaded outlet cover on any bathroom outlet that isn't in daily use is a low-effort safety layer, separate from GFCI protection — the two aren't substitutes for each other. If the outlet is also visually cluttered with cords, a <a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0GS4W3D7J" target="_blank" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener">cable-management outlet cover</a> solves both problems at once by giving plugs and slack cord somewhere to sit behind a closed plate.

## Don't Route Cords Near the Tub or Sink

Any cord — charger, dryer, trimmer — should route away from the sink and tub, not across or above them. This is less about tidiness and more about keeping cords out of splash range entirely.

## The Bottom Line

GFCI protection is the non-negotiable part; cord covers and outlet plates are the layer on top that keeps daily use tidy and keeps small hands away from outlets that don't need to be accessible.
