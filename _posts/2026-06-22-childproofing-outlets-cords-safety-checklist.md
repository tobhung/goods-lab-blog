Cable management for a home with a toddler is a different problem than cable management for a home office — the goal isn't just tidiness, it's keeping curious hands away from outlets and cords entirely. Here's a practical checklist, not a product dump.

## Outlets First

Basic plug-in outlet caps are better than nothing, but they're also small, easy to remove, and easy for a toddler to swallow if popped out. For outlets that are actually in use — behind a TV stand, next to a nightstand — a sliding-plate cover that stays attached to the wall and only opens when you push a plug in and slide it is a meaningfully safer option than caps that come out entirely.

## Cords Are the Bigger Risk

Outlets get the attention, but a dangling cord within reach — a lamp cord, a phone charger hanging off a nightstand — is often the bigger hazard, both as a strangulation risk and simply something to pull on and bring a device crashing down. A cord safety case or child-protect cord cover encloses the cord in a rigid or semi-rigid sleeve that a toddler can't easily bite through or wrap around anything.

## Room-by-Room Priorities

- **Nursery**: Every outlet within reach of the crib or changing table should have a sliding-plate cover, and no cords — monitor cables, lamp cords — should hang lower than crib-rail height.
- **Living room**: TV and entertainment center cords are usually the largest cluster in the house. Bundle them into a single rigid cord cover or channel along the baseboard, out of reach of a crawling child.
- **Kitchen**: Small appliance cords (kettle, toaster) should never dangle over a counter edge where a pull could bring the appliance down.

## What to Buy vs. What to DIY

Rigid cord covers and sliding-plate outlet covers are worth buying rather than improvising — they're inexpensive, and a DIY tape-and-towel solution doesn't hold up to a determined two-year-old. Where you can save effort is on non-hazard areas: an outlet behind a heavy bookshelf a child can't reach doesn't need the same treatment as one at floor level in an open room.

## A Reminder, Not a Guarantee

No cord safety case or outlet cover replaces supervision — they reduce risk, they don't eliminate it. Treat this checklist as your baseline, then walk your home at your child's eye level (literally get down on the floor) to catch what a standing adult's view misses.
