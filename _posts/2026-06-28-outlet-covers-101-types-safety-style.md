"Outlet cover" means several different products depending on what problem you're solving — safety, style, or cable clutter. Here's the difference, since buying the wrong type is a common and easily avoidable mistake.

## Standard Flat Covers

This is what ships with most homes: a flat plastic or metal plate with cutouts for the outlet and a center screw. Its only job is covering the wiring box. No cavity, no safety mechanism, purely functional.

## Decorative Covers

Same flat design, different materials and finishes — brushed metal, wood-look, colors that match a specific wall paint. These exist purely for style and don't change how the outlet functions at all.

## Child-Safety Covers

Sliding-plate covers stay attached to the wall and only expose the outlet when you actively push a plug in and slide the cover open, unlike simple plug-in caps that can be pulled out entirely. If there are small children in the house, this category matters more than any other on this list — see our full childproofing checklist for room-by-room guidance.

## Cable-Management Covers

This is the newer category: a deeper plate with a built-in cavity behind it, designed to hold plug heads and slack cord out of view. ## How to Pick

Match the cover to the actual problem at that specific outlet:

- Outlet behind furniture, purely functional → standard cover, don't overspend
- Outlet within reach of a small child → sliding-plate safety cover
- Outlet with visible cord clutter (desk, nightstand, counter) → cable-management cover
- Outlet in a highly visible spot where finish matters → decorative cover

## One Cover Doesn't Have to Do Everything

It's tempting to look for a single product that's safe, stylish, and manages cables all at once. In practice, most outlets only have one real problem to solve at a time — pick the category that matches the actual issue at that specific outlet rather than over-buying for a problem you don't have there.
