Minimalist doesn't mean empty — it means every item earns its spot. Cable management fits naturally into that philosophy, since the goal is the same either way: remove what doesn't need to be visible, and make what's left intentional.

## Fewer, Better Pieces

A minimalist desk setup usually replaces five small, mismatched clips and organizers with two or three pieces that do more per item. A single cable tray under the desk, one dedicated cord spine along a leg, and one outlet cover with a built-in cavity often replace an entire drawer of miscellaneous clips accumulated over years.

## Color and Material Discipline

The fastest way to undercut a minimalist look is a rainbow of charging cables and clear plastic organizers sitting on top of a carefully chosen wood or matte-finish desk. Swap visible cables for black or neutral-colored versions where you can, and pick accessories in one consistent material family so nothing reads as an afterthought.

## What to Remove Entirely

Before adding new aesthetic desk accessories, remove what's not doing anything:

- Duplicate chargers you don't need at the desk
- Cable clips holding cords you could route out of sight entirely
- Empty adapter boxes and unused dongles living in a drawer "just in case"

## The One Splurge Worth It

If there's one item worth spending more on, it's whatever handles the outlet itself — that's the one visual element sitting at floor or desk level that's genuinely hard to hide with clips or a tray. A cable-management outlet cover keeps the plugs and slack contained, which matters more in a minimalist setup than a maximalist one, since there's nothing else around it to visually compete with the clutter.

## The Test for "Enough"

A minimalist desk isn't measured by how few items you own — it's whether you could describe every visible object's purpose in one sentence. If you can do that for your desk right now, you're done. If not, that's your list for this weekend.
