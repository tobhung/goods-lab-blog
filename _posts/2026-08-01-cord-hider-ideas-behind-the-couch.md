The area behind a couch collects more cords than people expect — a lamp, a router, maybe a console or streaming box on a side table — and most of it is invisible until you actually look. Here's how to deal with each piece without moving the furniture out first.

## Lamp and Side Table Cords

Run these along the back leg of the furniture with adhesive clips rather than letting them cross open floor behind the couch. A single clip every 8–10 inches keeps the cord flat against the leg instead of sagging into view from the side.

## Router and Modem Placement

If the router sits behind the couch for Wi-Fi coverage reasons, give it its own small shelf or stand rather than setting it directly on the floor — floor-level placement means more cord slack pooling where you can see it, and worse signal besides.

## The Couch-to-Wall Gap

For a couch pushed close to the wall, a flat cord cover strip laid along the baseboard behind it routes everything toward the nearest outlet without needing anything mounted on the wall itself — the couch hides the strip completely.

## When the Couch Floats in the Room

If the couch isn't against a wall, route cords under it instead of behind it. A low-profile under-furniture cable channel keeps cords off the visible floor on either side, which matters more in a floating layout than a wall-backed one.

## The Takeaway

Most behind-the-couch clutter is solvable with clips and a floor-level cord cover rather than anything mounted on the wall — save the wall channel for cords that have to cross open wall space with no furniture to hide behind.
