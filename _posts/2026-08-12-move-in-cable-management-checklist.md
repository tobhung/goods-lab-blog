Cable management is easy to forget in the middle of a move, but it's genuinely easier to set up before furniture is in its final position than after. This is the order that avoids redoing work later.

## Before Furniture Arrives

- **Test every outlet** in the rooms you'll actually use, so you know which ones are dead before you're relying on them for a TV or router setup.
- **Note outlet placement relative to where furniture will go** — this decides whether a cord will need to cross open floor or can route directly behind a couch or desk.

## While Setting Up the TV and Entertainment Area

- **Mount the TV before running any cords**, so you're routing to the actual final position rather than guessing and redoing it.
- **Pick renter-friendly options first** — adhesive cord covers and clips remove cleanly, which matters if you don't yet know how long you'll be in the unit.

## Setting Up the Home Office Corner

- **Route the router and modem cords before the desk goes against the wall**, since moving a desk to fix a cord routing mistake is more work than doing it once, correctly, at move-in.
- **Use a power strip mounted under the desk** rather than one sitting on the floor, from day one — retrofitting this after you've settled in usually means unplugging and rerouting everything.

## Outlets in High-Traffic Areas

- **Add a cable-management outlet cover** anywhere a power strip or multiple plugs will sit permanently visible, like a <a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0GS4W3D7J" target="_blank" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener">Refiner cable management outlet cover</a> — it's a five-minute install now versus retrofitting around furniture later.
- **Cover any outlet you won't use regularly**, especially if kids or pets will be in the space.

## The Takeaway

Cable management done during a move takes minutes per item; the same fixes done after you're settled in usually mean moving furniture back out of the way first. Handle it in the order above and you won't have to.
