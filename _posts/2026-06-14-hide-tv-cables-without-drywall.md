The cleanest-looking TV setups usually aren't hiding cables inside the wall — that's an in-wall power kit, an electrician, and a permanent modification most renters simply can't do. The good news is a tv mount cable concealer gets you 90% of that clean look with none of the commitment.

## Why In-Wall Isn't Always the Answer

Routing cables inside the wall cavity is the "invisible" option, but it requires cutting drywall, fishing cable through studs, and in most areas, a separate in-wall-rated power cable if you want the outlet moved behind the TV. It's a real project, and it's not reversible when you move out.

## The Surface-Mount Alternative

A paintable cable channel or raceway mounts flat against the wall and can be painted to match, making it disappear at normal viewing distance. It typically comes in a few pieces:

1. A vertical channel running from the TV down to outlet height
2. Clips or adhesive backing to hold it flush against the wall
3. A cover strip that snaps on after the cables are laid in

Because it's surface-mounted, there's no drywall work — just cleaning the wall, applying the adhesive strip, and pressing the channel into place.

## Handling the Outlet End

The other half of the problem is what happens where the cables meet the wall outlet. A tangle of a power brick, an HDMI switch, and a streaming device power cord right next to the outlet undoes a lot of the clean look you just created above it. This is where a deeper wall outlet cover with a built-in cavity is worth pairing with your cable channel — it gives the plugs and slack a place to tuck away instead of dangling at the bottom of your neat vertical run.

## A Few Practical Notes

- Measure the full run from TV to outlet before ordering a channel kit — most come in fixed lengths and you may need two.
- If you're hiding an HDMI cable, check the connector width fits inside the channel before you commit to a paint job.
- For renters, stick to adhesive-backed or clip-mounted systems only — anything requiring wall anchors should get a quick check with your lease terms first.

Done right, this whole project takes an afternoon and is completely reversible if you move.
