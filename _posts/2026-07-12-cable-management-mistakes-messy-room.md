Most cable management failures aren't about not trying — they're a handful of repeated mistakes that undo otherwise decent effort. Here's what to watch for.

## Mistake 1: Bundling by Type Instead of Destination

Grouping "all power cables" together and "all data cables" together seems organized, but if a monitor's power and video cables end up in two different bundles, you've created two things to manage instead of one. Bundle by where the cables are going together, not by category.

## Mistake 2: Buying Storage for the Wrong Amount of Cable

A cable box or tray sized for three cords doesn't help if you actually have seven. Count what you're really managing before buying containment — undersized storage just becomes visible overflow again within a month.

## Mistake 3: Ignoring the Outlet

Cords get routed, bundled, and hidden along their length, and then dumped in a visible tangle right at the wall outlet because that last six inches felt like a solved problem. It isn't — a wall outlet cover with a built-in cavity is a small, specific fix for exactly this gap.

## Mistake 4: Mixing Materials and Colors

A room can have every cord perfectly routed and still look cluttered if there's a rainbow of cable colors and three different clip materials on display. Visual noise is still noise, even when it's technically organized.

## Mistake 5: No Slack Plan

Cables need some slack for moving furniture or devices, but unplanned slack becomes the coiled mess that sits on top of otherwise good cable management. Decide where slack lives — inside a cable box, coiled behind furniture, tucked into an outlet cover cavity — rather than letting it pool wherever there's room.

## Mistake 6: Treating It as a One-Time Project

New devices get added, chargers get swapped, and a cable setup that was clean six months ago slowly drifts back toward clutter as each new cable gets added without re-checking the system. A five-minute quarterly check — is everything still routed the way it was set up to be — catches this before it becomes a full redo.

## The Common Thread

Every mistake on this list comes from treating cable management as a single action instead of an ongoing system. Fix the system once, and the daily maintenance is nearly nothing.
