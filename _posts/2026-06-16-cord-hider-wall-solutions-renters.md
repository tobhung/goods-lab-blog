If you rent, "just run the cable through the wall" isn't really on the table. Every cord hider wall solution below works without drilling into studs, patching drywall, or doing anything your landlord would notice at move-out inspection.

## Adhesive Cable Channels

These are the most common cord hider wall product on the market: a paintable plastic or silicone channel that sticks to the wall with a strong adhesive backing. They're removable with minimal residue if you use a hairdryer to soften the adhesive before peeling. Good for routing a cord vertically from a wall-mounted shelf or TV down to an outlet.

## Cord Covers for Baseboards

For cords running horizontally along the floor — think a lamp cord crossing a room to reach an outlet — a low-profile baseboard-style cord cover that clips over the existing baseboard (rather than attaching to the wall itself) avoids any adhesive or hardware on painted surfaces entirely.

## Outlet-Level Cord Hiders

The part most renters overlook is the outlet itself. Swapping in a deeper wall plate with a built-in cavity — like the <a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0GS4W3D7J" target="_blank" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener">Refiner cable management outlet cover</a> — takes two minutes with a screwdriver, uses the exact same screw holes as your existing cover, and gives you a place to tuck plug heads and slack cord right at the source. Since it's a straight swap of the cover plate, not a wiring change, most leases have no issue with it, and you can put the original cover back before you move.

## What to Check Before Buying Anything

- Confirm any adhesive product is rated for your wall finish — textured or freshly painted walls can be less forgiving.
- Measure twice: cord hider wall channels are usually sold in fixed lengths, and outlet covers are sized for standard single-gang outlets.
- Keep the original hardware. Whatever you remove — a stock outlet cover, a baseboard piece — bag it and label it so move-out is a five-minute job, not a scavenger hunt.

None of these require a landlord's permission in most standard leases, but if you're unsure, a quick text asking "can I swap the outlet cover plate, keeping the original to reinstall" is a fast way to confirm.
