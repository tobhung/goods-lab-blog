A cable concealer kit is really just a cord hider wall channel sold specifically for the TV-to-outlet run, but the kits vary more than the marketing photos suggest. Here's what actually matters when you're picking one.

## Channel Width and Cord Capacity

Add up what's actually running down the wall — power cord, HDMI, maybe an Ethernet cable for a streaming box — and check the kit's rated capacity before buying. Undersized channels either won't close flush or force you to leave one cord snaking around the outside, which defeats the point.

## Paintable vs. Pre-Colored

A paintable, textured-primer channel is the safer default unless you can find a pre-colored option in your exact wall shade. Pre-colored kits only look seamless when the match is close; anything off by more than a shade reads as a plastic strip stuck to the wall.

## Adhesive vs. Screw-Mounted

Adhesive kits are the renter-friendly choice and remove cleanly if the backing is good, but they're weaker on textured or recently painted walls where the finish hasn't fully cured. Screw-mounted channels hold better long-term and are the better pick on drywall you're allowed to put small holes in.

## Length and Corners

Measure the actual run, including any turn from the TV down to the outlet, and buy a kit with pre-cut corner pieces rather than one you're expected to notch yourself with a utility knife — factory corners sit flush; hand-cut ones usually don't.

## The Bottom Line

For most wall-mounted TVs, a mid-capacity paintable channel with adhesive backing and a factory corner piece covers the job without over-buying. Skip the capacity upgrade unless you're also running a soundbar or extra streaming hardware through the same channel.
