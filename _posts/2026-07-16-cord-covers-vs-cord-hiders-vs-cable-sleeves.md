These three terms get used almost interchangeably in product listings, which makes shopping for the right one harder than it should be. Here's what actually distinguishes them.

## Cord Covers

Typically a low-profile channel designed to protect a cord running across a floor or along a baseboard — think a cord crossing a walkway where it could be a trip hazard. The priority is protection and safety underfoot, not necessarily hiding the cord from view, though many do both.

## Cord Hiders

A broader category that includes wall-mounted channels, raceways, and paintable covers meant to make a cord visually disappear against a wall or floor. The priority here is aesthetics — a cord hider is judged by how invisible it makes the cord look, more than by physical protection.

## Cable Sleeves

A flexible fabric or neoprene tube, usually zippered or wrapped in a spiral, that bundles multiple loose cables into a single, neater cylinder. Unlike the first two, a sleeve doesn't attach to a wall or floor — it travels with the cables themselves, which makes it the right choice for cords that move, like behind a desktop PC you occasionally pull out to access.

## Matching the Product to the Problem

- Cord crossing a floor where people walk → **cord cover** (protection first)
- Cord running along a wall you want to visually disappear → **cord hider** (aesthetics first)
- A bundle of loose cables that stay mostly in one place but need consolidating → **cable sleeve**

## Where People Get It Wrong

The most common mistake is buying a decorative cord hider for a cord that's actually a trip hazard underfoot — it looks fine but doesn't hold up to being stepped on repeatedly. The reverse mistake is using a heavy-duty floor cord cover on a wall-mounted cord, which is usually bulkier and less visually clean than a purpose-built wall hider.

## The Outlet Is a Fourth Category

None of these three address the outlet itself, where cords actually originate. That's a separate product — a wall plate with a built-in cavity — and it's worth treating as its own decision rather than expecting a cord cover or hider to solve the mess right at the plug.
