There's no shortage of cord hider products on the market, but a lot of them solve a problem you don't have while ignoring the one you do. These seven earn a spot because each targets a specific, common clutter point.

## 1. Adhesive Wall Cable Channels

The standard cord hider wall product — a paintable raceway that mounts flat against the wall to route a cord vertically or horizontally without cutting into drywall.

## 2. Under-Desk Cable Trays

Mounts to the underside of a desk and holds a power strip and cord slack above the floor. Solves the "pile of cords under the desk" problem specifically, and does nothing for cords elsewhere — which is fine, since that's not what it's for.

## 3. Baseboard Cord Covers

A low-profile channel that clips over an existing baseboard to route a cord across a room without adhesive or wall damage — ideal for a lamp or appliance cord that has to cross open floor.

## 4. Cable Sleeves

A zippered or velcro fabric sleeve that bundles several loose cables into one neat cylinder. Best for a bundle of cords running the same direction, like from a desktop PC to a monitor and speakers.

## 5. Cable-Management Outlet Covers

This is the one most lists skip: a deeper wall plate with a built-in cavity for plugs and slack cord, installed at the outlet itself. The <a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0GS4W3D7J" target="_blank" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener">Refiner cable management outlet cover</a> is a good example — it's the only item on this list that addresses the outlet directly rather than routing cord toward or away from it.

## 6. Cord Clips and Cable Ties

The cheapest and lowest-commitment option: adhesive or screw-mounted clips that hold one or two cords along a surface edge. Not glamorous, but effective for keeping a single cord from sliding off the back of a desk.

## 7. Cable Boxes

A vented box that a power strip and its connected adapters sit inside, hiding the whole tangle at once. Best for a cluster of adapters and a power strip that would otherwise sit in the open on a floor or shelf.

## Picking From the List

Match the product to where the mess actually is rather than buying the most popular item across the board — a cable box under a desk does nothing for cords at a nightstand, and an outlet cover does nothing for a tangle of cords three feet away on the floor. Most homes end up using two or three of these, not just one.
