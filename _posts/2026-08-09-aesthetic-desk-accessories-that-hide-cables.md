Most desk accessories are chosen for looks, and most cable management is chosen for function — but a handful of items do both jobs at once, which means one purchase instead of two competing for the same desk space.

## Monitor Stands With a Cable Shelf

A raised monitor stand with an open shelf underneath does double duty: it lifts the screen to eye level and gives cords, a keyboard, or small accessories somewhere to sit instead of trailing across the desk surface.

## Woven or Ceramic Cable Boxes

A power strip hidden inside a woven basket or ceramic-look box reads as a styling object rather than a tech accessory, and works on any desk where the outlet cluster would otherwise sit exposed in the open.

## Desk Pads With Integrated Cable Channels

Some desk pads now come with a built-in channel along the back edge that a keyboard or mouse cord tucks into, keeping the pad's surface clean without adding a separate clip or tray.

## Wooden or Stone Cable Clips

A small weighted clip in wood or stone holds a single cord flat against the desk edge and looks more like a paperweight than a cable accessory — a better fit for a desk built around natural materials than a plastic clip would be.

## Desk Organizer Trays With a Charging Cutout

A desk tray with a built-in slot for a charging cable lets a phone charge in its usual spot without the cord crossing the tray's open storage area, which keeps the whole surface looking intentional rather than improvised.

## The Bottom Line

The best aesthetic desk accessories for cable management are the ones where hiding the cord is the accessory's actual job, not an afterthought — a cable box that looks nice is still a cable box, and it'll hold up better than decor items awkwardly repurposed to hide cords.
