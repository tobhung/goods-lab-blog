By the time a baby becomes a mobile toddler, the list of things worth baby-proofing has grown a lot — and cords and outlets are near the top, because they're everywhere and a toddler's curiosity doesn't discriminate between a lamp cord and a toy.

## The Two Categories You Actually Need

Baby-proofing cords and outlets breaks down into exactly two categories, and it's worth understanding both rather than buying whatever's cheapest.

**Outlet covers** stop small fingers (and small objects) from going into the outlet itself. Sliding-plate covers that stay attached to the wall are safer long-term than plug-in caps, which are small enough to be a choking hazard if a toddler manages to pop one out.

**Cord safety cases** enclose an in-use cord along its length, so a toddler can't chew on it, pull it, or wrap it around something. This matters most for cords that run at floor or crawling height — lamp cords, phone chargers left plugged in near the floor, holiday lighting.

## A Room-by-Room Pass

- **Living room**: TV and entertainment center cords are usually the densest cluster in the house and the easiest to miss, since they're often behind furniture where a toddler can still reach but you don't look often. Route everything into a rigid cord cover, not just a soft bundle.
- **Bedrooms**: Nightstand lamp cords and any charger left plugged in near the floor should be enclosed, and outlets within reach of a crib should get sliding-plate covers, not caps.
- **Kitchen**: Countertop appliance cords shouldn't dangle below the counter edge, and any floor-level outlets should be covered even if they're rarely used — toddlers find outlets adults forgot existed.

## What's Worth Buying vs. Skipping

Sliding-plate outlet covers and rigid cord safety cases are worth the money — they're inexpensive relative to the risk they address, and they hold up to repeated tugging in a way improvised solutions don't. You can skip decorative or cable-management-style outlet covers in rooms where a toddler has access; safety covers should take priority there, and aesthetics can wait for the outlets they can't reach.

## Supervision Still Matters

None of this replaces keeping an eye on a mobile toddler — it reduces the risk during the moments you can't be watching every second, which in a busy household is most of them.
