A nightstand usually has the same cable problem as a desk, just shrunk down: a lamp, a phone charger, maybe a white-noise machine or humidifier, all sharing one nearby outlet. Because it's the last thing you see before sleep and the first thing you see waking up, it's worth getting right.

## Identify What's Actually Plugged In

Most nightstands are managing two to four cords: a lamp, a phone charger, and occasionally a second device. Before buying anything, confirm the count — it determines whether you need a small cord hider or something with more capacity.

## Route Cords Behind, Not Around

If your nightstand has any open back or a gap against the wall, route cords through that gap rather than around the visible sides. A small adhesive clip on the inside back panel keeps cords from sliding to the front where they're visible from the bed.

## The Wall-to-Outlet Gap

If your nightstand sits away from the wall outlet, or the cord has to travel visibly along the wall to reach it, a short cord hider wall channel handles that run cleanly — it's a small-scale version of the same product used behind a mounted TV, just shorter.

## Fix the Outlet Itself

The outlet behind a nightstand is a near-perfect use case for a cable-management outlet cover: it's low-profile, it's usually the only cover in the room anyone will actually look at closely, and a lamp plus a charger is exactly the amount of slack cord these covers are designed to hold. It also happens to double as one of the more effective aesthetic desk accessories for a room where "desk" is really a small bedside surface.

## Charging Without Adding a Cord

If your phone supports it, a wireless charging pad on the nightstand surface removes one cord from the equation entirely, leaving only the lamp cord to manage — worth considering before you solve a problem you could just remove.

## The Bedtime Test

At night, in low light, reaching for your phone or turning off the lamp shouldn't involve feeling around for a cord or knocking something over. If it currently does, that's the sign your nightstand cable management needs the pass described above, not just a tidier-looking surface.
