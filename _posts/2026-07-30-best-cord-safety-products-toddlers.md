Once a baby starts crawling and pulling up on furniture, cords go from background clutter to an active hazard within about a week. These are the specific product categories worth having in the house, not just a general "childproof the outlets" reminder.

## Rigid Cord Covers

A hard plastic channel that clips over an in-use cord along its full length, preventing a toddler from biting through it, chewing the insulation, or getting it wrapped around a hand or neck. This matters most for cords at floor or crawling height — lamp cords, extension cords, holiday lighting left out longer than planned.

## Cord Shorteners and Winders

Rather than covering excess cord, a cord winder takes up the slack so there's less length available to pull on in the first place. This is especially useful for appliance cords in the kitchen or lamp cords where the outlet is much closer than the cord length assumes.

## Sliding-Plate Outlet Covers

Unlike small plug-in caps — which are a choking hazard themselves once removed — a sliding-plate cover stays permanently attached to the wall and only exposes the outlet when a plug is inserted and slid into position. This is the safer default for any outlet within a toddler's reach.

## Cord Safety Cases for Electronics

Power strips and multi-device charging stations benefit from an enclosed case that covers the strip itself along with the plugged-in cord ends, since a power strip at floor level is often more accessible — and more interesting to a toddler — than a single outlet.

## Furniture-Anchoring Straps (A Related, Often-Missed Item)

Not strictly a cord product, but cords are frequently what pulls a lamp or light furniture over when tugged — anchoring straps for anything a cord is attached to reduce the consequence even if a cord does get pulled.

## Buying Priorities

If you're starting from zero, prioritize in this order: sliding-plate covers for reachable outlets, rigid cord covers for floor-level cords in main living areas, then cord winders and cases for the rest. All of these are inexpensive relative to the risk, and none of them are a substitute for keeping an eye on a mobile toddler — they reduce risk during the moments supervision inevitably lapses.
